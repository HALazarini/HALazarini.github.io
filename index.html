Working Papers

Pollster Competition and Its Electoral Effects

(with Marcos Ross Fernandes)

Presented at: 48th Meeting of the Brazilian Econometric Society (SBE) – forthcoming

: Pollsters are often cited as main players during campaigns and as major trendsetters for electoral outcomes, but little study has been made to understand how they affected by market-forces. In this paper, we provide a partial equilibrium model to study how individual pollsters make rational decisions to maximize profit in response to market structures and how those decisions affect overall poll precision. We show that market equilibrium is not always efficient, especially in the presence of asymmetric cost parameters or correlation among polls. We analyze a policy intervention in which a minimum level of precision is required for polls to hit the market, and conclude that it can have significant effects on reallocating welfare, increasing efficiency, and changing market concentration. We then introduce the behavioral element of correlation neglect and show that there are parameters under which it induces pollsters to make more precise polls and ultimately increase welfare.
JEL Codes: D83, D72, D40.

Legislative Bargaining with Endogenous Feasibility Restriction

(with Wilfredo Maldonado)

Presented at: 4th International Workshop of Game Theory and Economic Applications (IWGTEA)

: This study proposes a legislative bargaining model in which allocations impact growth, hence impacting the size of the available budget in the future. Compared to the original bargaining models (Baron and Ferejohn, 1989; Baron, 1996; Rubinstein, 1982; Banks and Duggan, 2000), this model has three main contributions: it allows for the political process to impact growth, and it explains both small proposer premium and the existence of supermajorities. A simple example is provided to show that this framework can have small proposer premiums and supermajorities. A generalized version of the model is characterized, and under certain hypothesis equilibrium is proven to exist. Some conditions are described under which the solution might be unique. Extensions are also suggested. To the best of our knowledge, this model is the first to explicitly endogenize growth as a result of game-theoretic political interaction.
JEL Codes: D72, O43, D61, D71.

The Effects of a Fancy Degree: Evidences from Brazil

(with Marcos Simão Coelho and Paula Pereda)

: The value of higher education can be perceived through many different lenses. While there are important results on human capital accumulation and network connections, we propose studying the effects derived solely from graduating from a renowned university - a “prestige effect”. We use novel data to exploit the incorporation of the Engineering School of Lorena by the University of Sao Paulo, one of the most renowned universities in Latin America, in 2006; allowing us to compare the career paths of individuals who had been selected through the same screening procedure while entering university and were trained by the same pool of professors, but ended up receiving different degrees after graduating depending on whether they graduated before or after the incorporation. We propose a regression discontinuity design to compare the lifetime earnings of students that graduated close to the incorporation, and also apply an event study to understand possible heterogeneous effects over time. We expect to show that a renowned degree gives a good head-start in the graduate's early career, but since individuals in the control and treatment groups are not systematically different regarding background or human capital, their earnings should show some signs of convergence in their late career.
JEL Codes: I23, C24.
